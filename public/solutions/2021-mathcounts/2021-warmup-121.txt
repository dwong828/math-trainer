Step 1: Set Up the Right Triangle
Let the radius of Circle $P$ be $r_1 = 18$ meters, and the radius of Circle $Q$ be $r_2 = 32$ meters.

Distance between centers ($PQ$):
Since the two circles are externally tangent to each other, the distance between their centers is the sum of their radii:
$PQ = r_1 + r_2 = 18 + 32 = 50$

Form a right triangle:
Draw segment $PX$ perpendicular to line $XY$ and segment $QY$ perpendicular to line $XY$.

Construct a rectangle $XYHZ$ by drawing a line segment from center $P$ perpendicular to line segment $QY$, intersecting $QY$ at point $H$.

In right triangle $\triangle PHQ$:
The hypotenuse is $PQ = 50$.
One leg is $PH = XY$ (the length of the common tangent).
The other leg is $QH = r_2 - r_1 = 32 - 18 = 14$.

Step 2: Apply the Pythagorean Theorem
$\text{In } \triangle PHQ: \quad PH^2 + QH^2 = PQ^2$

$(XY)^2 + 14^2 = 50^2$

$(XY)^2 + 196 = 2500$

$(XY)^2 = 2500 - 196 = 2304$

$XY = \sqrt{2304} = 48$

Alternative Direct Formula
For two externally tangent circles with radii $r_1$ and $r_2$, the length of their common external tangent segment is given directly by:

$XY = 2\sqrt{r_1 r_2} = 2\sqrt{18 \times 32} = 2\sqrt{576} = 2 \times 24 = 48$

The length of $XY$ is 48 meters.
